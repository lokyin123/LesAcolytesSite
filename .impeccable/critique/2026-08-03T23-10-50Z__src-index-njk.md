## Design Specificity Verdict

**Moderately specific in visual language; weakly specific in homepage storytelling.**

The ensemble photography, four-role typography, muted palette, and alternating split panels produce a recognisable contemporary editorial character. The homepage sequence itself—hero, social links, then seven equal destination panels—is category-interchangeable. A theatre company, architecture studio, or arts festival could reuse it with minimal change. It communicates “cultured arts organisation” more strongly than this particular French and French-adjacent repertoire ensemble.

The deterministic scan reported one warning in `src/index.njk:118`: `broken-image` on the lightbox placeholder `<img data-gallery-expanded src="">`. Lifecycle inspection shows this is probably not user-visible: the gallery script assigns a real source before opening the dialog and clears it after close. The scan is syntactically correct but lacks runtime context. Omitting the initial `src` and removing the attribute on close would eliminate the warning cleanly.

No browser-automation interface was available, so there is no user-visible detector overlay. Evidence came from the CLI detector, source inspection, and HTTP 200 rendered-HTML checks.

## Overall Impression

The opening is assured, warm, and photographically credible. The largest opportunity is editorial rather than decorative: turn the homepage from an oversized site map into a curated first encounter with the music. The emotional arc currently peaks in the hero, drops into unfinished social destinations, then settles into a long sequence of mechanically equal route panels.

## What's Working

1. **The hero establishes atmosphere quickly.** The full-height photo-and-colour split gives the ensemble presence without sales-funnel theatrics.
2. **The type system feels authored.** Major Mono Display, Bricolage Grotesque, Spectral, and Manrope have distinct and useful roles.
3. **Destination panels are generous and usable.** Each is a large link with focus treatment, restrained motion, responsive stacking, and lazy-loaded imagery.

## Cognitive Load

**Moderate: three checklist failures.** Individual sections have clear focus and grouping, but the page fails chunking, minimal choices, and one-thing-at-a-time flow. Desktop navigation exposes seven peers and the page repeats the same seven choices. The hero promises ensemble discovery, then immediately presents four external platforms instead of advancing the ensemble story.

## Emotional Journey

The hero creates a strong peak. “Find us on” is an emotional valley because it prioritises incomplete external profiles over music or people. Repeated split panels maintain polish but flatten anticipation. Contact provides a logical ending, although reaching it requires excessive scrolling.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — The hero CTA makes a false promise

**What:** “Discover the ensemble” scrolls to “Find us on,” not ensemble information.

**Why it matters:** This is the first explicit action. A visitor expects story, musicians, or sound and instead receives four exits from the site.

**Fix:** Link to About, or replace the next section with a concise ensemble introduction and purposeful routes into About or Musicians. If social discovery is intended, relabel the CTA honestly.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### P1 — Unfinished social links occupy the highest-value post-hero position

**What:** Facebook and Continuo point to generic destinations while being labelled “coming soon.”

**Why it matters:** The page foregrounds unfinished profiles rather than musical proof, weakening trust for concertgoers and promoters.

**Fix:** Remove or disable unavailable destinations. Promote a genuine recording, upcoming performance, or ensemble introduction after the hero; retain validated social links in a quieter later position.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### P1 — The homepage hides the highest-intent audience needs

**What:** Concertgoers see no upcoming date, and promoters see no programme, recording, venue proof, or booking path in the opening sequence.

**Why it matters:** These audiences are equally central in `PRODUCT.md`, but the homepage treats every route as equally important.

**Fix:** Surface a compact next-performance module and one promoter-oriented proof/action near the top, using confirmed data and an editorial rather than sales-led tone.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### P2 — Seven full-scale panels turn navigation into page length

**What:** Every secondary route receives a panel at least 560px high on desktop.

**Why it matters:** Equal scale implies equal importance, erases rhythm, and makes imagery feel repetitive after the first few panels.

**Fix:** Keep full-scale treatment for three primary stories, then group remaining destinations into a denser editorial index. Let events, recordings, people, and articles use content-specific modules.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### P2 — The visual identity is stronger than the musical identity

**What:** The homepage contains no composer, work, instrument, programme title, upcoming date, or playable musical object beyond “French Baroque.”

**Why it matters:** Visitors understand the site’s taste before they understand the ensemble’s substance.

**Fix:** Introduce one confirmed musical object—a genuine recording, programme, composer, or upcoming performance—without inventing claims.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan — First-time visitor

- “Discover the ensemble” lands on social channels, making the intended journey unclear.
- Seven equal navigation choices and seven equal panels provide visibility without guidance about where to begin.
- The hero explains the repertoire category but does not offer anything immediate to hear, see, or attend.

### Riley — Deliberate stress tester

- Facebook and Continuo look actionable despite leading to generic, unfinished destinations.
- Reveal sections default to `opacity: 0`; a failed script can leave most homepage content invisible.
- The primary CTA label and destination describe different actions.
- The empty lightbox image source triggers a detector warning even though runtime behaviour probably prevents a visible broken image.

### Casey — Distracted mobile visitor

- The full-screen menu presents seven peers, then the page repeats all seven as tall stacked panels.
- The journey requires extensive one-handed scrolling before Contact.
- Two of the four compact social destinations are low-value dead ends.
- There is no homepage shortcut to the next performance.

### Promoter or presenter

- Programmes is visible but not privileged.
- No recording, recent venue, confirmed programme, or direct booking route appears near the opening.
- Contact sits at the end of the repeated panel sequence.

### Concertgoer

- No upcoming date or venue appears on the homepage.
- Platform-following is requested before answering “When can I hear the group?”

## Minor Observations

- Home is omitted from the main navigation, leaving no active route marker on the homepage.
- Empty alt text on linked panel images is defensible because the images are decorative and adjacent copy names each destination.
- The hover scale and heading shift are restrained and coherent.
- The 900px two-row header state may be dense, but this could not be screenshot-verified.
- Colour rotation provides atmosphere rather than semantic meaning.
- Slider and lightbox scripts load on the homepage even when their components are absent, adding avoidable mobile work.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the first scroll revealed the music rather than the ensemble’s social accounts?
- If only three destinations retained the large split-panel treatment, which three best express the ensemble’s priorities?
- Could a visitor answer “What do they sound like?” and “When can I hear them?” without leaving the homepage?
- What would make this composition impossible to mistake for another tasteful performing-arts website?
- Is the homepage a directory, or an authored performance of the ensemble’s identity?
